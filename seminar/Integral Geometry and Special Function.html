Hu Jie (JZU)

Laguerre (q-Laguerre) Weight Recurrence and Geometric Theory of Painleve equations

In this presentation, the connection between recurrence coefficients of classical and semi-classical orthogonal polynomials and solutions of the Painlevé equations is studied. We consider two classes of weight functions by geometric theory of Painleve equations. For the Laguerre weight, using Sakai's geometric theory of Painlevé equations, we reduce certain recurrence satisfied by associated recurrence coefficients to one of the canonical discrete Painlevé equation. For the deformed q-Laguerre weight, using Sakai's geometric theory of Painlevé equations, we present the identification procedure how the q-Laguerre weight recurrence is equivalent to the one of the discrete Painlevé equation.

Huang Yi (NJNU)

Beyond the solution of the Maxwell conjecture

The Maxwell conjecture is a 150-year-old idea in electrostatics stating that a system of n point charges in space can produce at most (n-1)² non-degenerate equilibrium (critical) points where the electric field cancels out to zero. The Maxwell Conjecture has been proven false in July 2026 by Arathoon, Ball, and Kvalheim. In this talk we shall discuss further interesting problems beyond their solution.

2026/11/20, 10:00 a.m., Tencent Meeting

Zhang Jiyuan (SCUT) Reviewer: Chen Zaoli (USTC)

Biorthogonal ensembles of derivative type

We prove that biorthogonal ensembles on the real line with a specific derivative structure admit an explicit correlation kernel of double contour integral form. We will demonstrate that this expression is a valuable starting point for asymptotic analysis and that our class of biorthogonal ensembles admits a large variety of limit kernels, by proving that two new classes of limit kernels can occur. The first type is a deformation of the hard edge Bessel kernel which arises in polynomial ensembles describing the eigenvalues of the sum of two random matrices, while the second type arises for Muttalib-Borodin type deformations of polynomial ensembles.

2026/12/04, 10:00 a.m., Tencent Meeting

Wang Haoming (NKU)

The Pearson family is characterized by Dixon-Anderson links on Gelfand-Tsetlin patterns

In this paper, we characterize the Pearson diffusion family by the Dixon--Anderson links on continuous Gelfand--Tsetlin patterns. Consider exchangeable diffusion generators

\[\mathcal L_n=\sum_{i=1}^n p(x_i)\partial_i^2+\sum_{i=1}^n\left[b_n(x_i)+r(x_i)\sum_{j\ne i}\frac1{x_i-x_j}\right]\partial_i.\]

If the Dixon--Anderson kernels satisfy

\[\mathcal L_{n+1}\Lambda_n^\theta = \Lambda_n^\theta \mathcal L_n\]

across levels, then necessarily

\[r=2\theta p,\quad p'''=0,\quad b_n''=0,\quad b_{n+1}=b_n-\theta p'.\]

Thus the generators are precisely of Pearson type. Conversely, every such Pearson generator satisfies the Dixon--Anderson intertwining relation. We also lift the generator identity to the associated Markov semigroups, recovering the classical Dyson, Laguerre, and Jacobi intertwinings and extending the Hua--Pickrell case to all (>0), including the colliding regime.

2026/12/20, 10:00 a.m., Tencent Meeting

Lyu Zhiyang (USTC)

The asymptotic behavior of Teichmüller rays

The asymptotic behavior of Teichmüller rays is a fundamental topic in Teichmüller geometry. In this talk, we investigate the asymptotic distance between two Teichmüller rays. While the divergent case has been understood through the works of Ivanov, Lenzhen and Masur, the bounded case remains more subtle. We focus on the bounded case and establish an explicit formula for the limiting distance between any two Teichmüller rays. Furthermore, we obtain a necessary and sufficient condition for two Teichmüller rays to be asymptotic. This is a joint work with Yi Qi.

2026/12/18, 10:00 a.m., Tencent Meeting

Wang Huaming (AHNU)

Biased random walk with traps

We consider a \(\lambda\)-biased random walk on the deterministic infinite tree \(\mathcal{T}=\{(i,j):i\ge0,\,0\le j\le i\}\), where \(\{(i,0):i\ge 0\}\) is the and, for each \(i\ge1\), \(\{(i,j):1\le j\le i\}\) is a attached to \((i,0)\). Traps cause long waiting times, leading to behavior distinct from simple random walks. The walk is recurrent for \(\lambda\ge1\) and transient for \(0<\lambda<1\). In the transient regime it is sub-ballistic: it goes to infinity with zero speed, and its distance from the root grows logarithmically, i.e.,\[\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{|X_n|}{\log n}=\frac{1}{\log(1/\lambda)},\quad\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{|X_n|}{\log n}=\frac{2}{\log(1/\lambda)},\quad\text{a.s.}.\] We also reveal a sharp contrast between spatial and temporal regeneration. Let \(C(n)\) be the number of cutpoints among the first \(n\) backbone vertices, and \(M(N)\) the number of cut times up to \(N\). Then \[\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{C(n)}{n}= 1-\lambda,\quad \lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{M(N)}{\log N}=(1-\lambda)\log(1/\lambda),\quad\text{a.s.},\] that is, cutpoints occur with positive linear density while cut times grow only logarithmically.

2027/01/08, 10:00 a.m., Tencent Meeting

Kang Mingyang (NKU) Reviewer:

A solution to Butler's positivity conjecture

Let \(\lambda, \mu, \nu\) be distinct partitions such that \(\lambda, \mu \subset \nu\) and \(|\nu/\lambda| = |\nu/\mu| = 1\). We prove Butler's positivity conjecture posed in 1994: the expansion of the Macdonald intersection polynomial \[\frac{T_{\lambda} \tilde{H}_{\mu}(X; q, t) - T_{\mu} \tilde{H}_{\lambda}(X; q, t)}{T_{\lambda} - T_{\mu}}\] in terms of the Schur function basis has coefficients in \(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}[q, t]\).
